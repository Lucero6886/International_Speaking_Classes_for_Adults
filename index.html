B1/B2 Speaking Lesson

Time Management & Priorities ⏳

Learn how to fluently discuss your daily schedule, organize your life, and express your opinions on productivity.

Today's Objectives:

  • Use advanced vocabulary related to daily routines and scheduling.
  • Answer personal questions fluently with extended details.
  • Express well-reasoned opinions about work-life balance.

☕ 1. Warm-up Discussion

Discuss these questions with your partner or teacher. Give detailed answers!

1. Are you a morning person or a night owl? 🦉

I am definitely a night owl. I focus best after 9 PM when the house is completely quiet.

2. Do you use a physical calendar or a digital to-do list? 📅

I rely on a digital calendar on my smartphone so I can get instant notifications.

3. Are you usually early, exactly on time, or late? ⏰

I make it a rule to be 5 minutes early. I really dislike making other people wait!

4. What is your busiest day of the week? 🏃

Monday is my busiest day because I have three back-to-back meetings.

📚 2. Essential Vocabulary

Learn these powerful words for discussing time and productivity.

Procrastinate (v)

To delay doing something that you should do, usually because you don't want to do it.

I always procrastinate when I have to write essays. I leave it until Sunday night.
Prioritize (v)

To decide which tasks are the most important so you can do them first.

When I have a heavy workload, I prioritize urgent tasks over checking emails.
Deadline (n)

A specific time or date by which something must be completely finished.

The final deadline for submitting the financial report is Friday at 5:00 PM.
Time-consuming (adj)

Taking a large amount of time to complete.

Organizing all these files manually is incredibly time-consuming.
Multitask (v)

To do several different things at the exact same time.

I try to multitask by listening to an English podcast while cooking dinner.
Overwhelmed (adj)

Feeling stressed because you have too much to handle or do.

I feel completely overwhelmed because I have three massive projects due this week.

🗣️ 3. Speaking Practice

Answer the questions. Use "Ideas" to brainstorm, and check "Sample Answers" if you get stuck.

EASY

1. How many hours of sleep do you usually get? Is it enough?

Ideas to talk about: 6-8 hours, feeling exhausted, needing more sleep, catching up on weekends.
Short Answer: I usually get around 7 hours of sleep, which is just enough for me.

Extended Answer: I aim for 8 hours, but typically I only get 6. Due to my demanding schedule, I often feel a bit exhausted during the week. I always try to sleep in on weekends to catch up.
MEDIUM

2. How do you remember important tasks or appointments?

Ideas to talk about: Phone alarms, Google Calendar, planners, sticky notes, asking someone to remind you.
Short Answer: I strictly use a planner app on my smartphone to keep track of things.

Extended Answer: I rely heavily on Google Calendar. Whenever I make an appointment, I log it into my phone immediately and set a reminder for one hour before. Without it, I would forget absolutely everything!
MEDIUM

3. What are the biggest distractions for you when you study or work?

Ideas to talk about: Smartphones, social media notifications, noisy environments, watching TV, feeling hungry.
Short Answer: My phone is my biggest distraction, especially Instagram and TikTok.

Extended Answer: Definitely my smartphone. Whenever I hear a notification, I glance at it, and then I end up mindlessly scrolling through social media for 20 minutes. I now try to leave my phone in another room when I need to focus.
OPINION

4. Is it better to focus on one single task, or to multitask?

Ideas to talk about: Higher quality work, fewer mistakes vs. getting more done quickly, feeling efficient.
Short Answer: I strongly believe focusing on one task is much better to ensure quality.

Extended Answer: Personally, I prefer to focus on a single task at a time. When I attempt to multitask, I feel like my attention is divided and I make silly mistakes. Focusing completely helps me finish tasks faster and with much higher quality.
OPINION

5. How important is work-life balance to you?

Ideas to talk about: Physical health, family time, preventing burnout, mental health, career sustainability.
Short Answer: It is absolutely essential for my mental well-being.

Extended Answer: Work-life balance is my ultimate priority. If I only focus on work, I quickly feel burned out and unmotivated. I maintain this balance by refusing to check work emails after 6 PM. Having personal time to recharge is crucial.

🎭 4. Role-Play Scenarios

Practice these real-life conversational situations with your partner.

Scenario A: Declining an Invitation

Situation: You are extremely busy with work and feel overwhelmed. A close friend invites you out for dinner tonight.

Task: Politely say no, explain exactly why you are busy, and suggest an alternative time to meet.

  • "I'd love to, but I'm completely overwhelmed with work right now."
  • "I have a strict deadline tomorrow morning."
  • "Can we take a raincheck on that?"
  • "How about next Friday instead? My schedule clears up then."

Scenario B: Planning a Project

Situation: You and a colleague need to organize a major presentation for next week.

Task: Discuss what needs to be done, prioritize the main tasks, and agree on deadlines for each other.

  • "First, we need to prioritize the research phase."
  • "I can handle the data collection. Will you design the slides?"
  • "Let's set a firm deadline for Wednesday at noon."
  • "That sounds like a solid plan to me."
