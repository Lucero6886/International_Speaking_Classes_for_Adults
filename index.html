2. Vocabulary 📖

10 useful words & phrases about privacy and digital well-being. Click a card to see an example sentence. (🔊 = say it twice for the class.)

data privacy
noun phrase
Protecting personal information from being accessed or shared without permission.
"I read the app's data privacy policy before signing up."
terms and conditions
phrase
The rules you agree to follow when you use a service or app.
"Hardly anyone actually reads the terms and conditions before clicking 'agree'."
screen time
noun
The amount of time you spend using a phone, tablet, or computer.
"My screen time went up a lot while I was working from home."
targeted ads
noun phrase
Advertisements chosen for you based on your online behaviour.
"I saw targeted ads for shoes right after I searched for them."
two-factor authentication
noun phrase
An extra security step that confirms your identity with a code.
"I turned on two-factor authentication to protect my banking app."
digital detox
noun
A period of time when someone avoids using electronic devices.
"We went on a digital detox during our trip to Hoi An."
cyberbullying
noun
Using the internet to hurt, threaten, or embarrass someone.
"Schools are teaching students how to report cyberbullying."
data breach
noun
An incident where private information is stolen or leaked.
"The company informed users after a data breach exposed their emails."
addictive
adjective
Making you want to keep using something, hard to stop.
"Short videos are designed to be addictive."
oversharing
noun
Giving too much personal information publicly, especially online.
"Oversharing on social media can put your safety at risk."

4. Role-Play 🎭

Two real-life situations. Read your role, then act it out with a partner. Click Show dialogue hints if you need support.

🎬 Scenario 1 — Deciding whether to download a new app

You and a friend want to download a new fitness app together, but it asks for access to your contacts, camera, and location all the time. Discuss whether to install it and find a compromise.

👤 Student A — Wants the convenience 👤 Student B — Worried about privacy
  • A: "Have you seen this app? It looks really useful for tracking our workouts together."
  • B: "Maybe, but look — it wants access to our contacts, camera, and location all the time."
  • A: "I know, but it would be so convenient. It could suggest routes and find friends nearby."
  • B: "I'd rather keep some privacy. Could we only allow location access while we're using it?"
  • A: "That's a fair compromise. Let's check the settings before we agree to anything."

🎬 Scenario 2 — Talking to a friend about their screen time

Your friend spends too much time on their phone and seems stressed. Suggest a digital detox plan and discuss the pros and cons together for 4–5 turns.

👤 Student A — Concerned friend 👤 Student B — Heavy phone user
  • A: "You seem really tired lately. How much time do you spend on your phone every day?"
  • B: "Probably too much, honestly — maybe six or seven hours, mostly scrolling."
  • A: "Have you thought about trying a digital detox, even just for a weekend?"
  • B: "I've thought about it, but I'm worried I'll miss something important."
  • A: "You could start small — no phone after 9 p.m., for example. Want to try it together?"
